01 · Overview

As considered as
the space itself

FOMO is a premium coworking and office campus in Sandnes, Norway. Four buildings, more than 200 companies, and a brand built around world-class design. The physical spaces are genuinely some of the nicest coworking environments in the region, and people are consistently impressed the moment they walk in.

The old website did not carry any of that. It felt dated and generic, the kind of site that could belong to any office rental company, and it quietly undersold the place it was meant to sell. The goal was direct: make the website look and feel like the space it is advertising, and make it easier to move through.

Starting point

If the website feels ordinary, it tells people the space is ordinary too. For a brand built on design, that gap was the whole problem.

02 · Approach

Designed
end to end

I designed the site end to end: the layout, the visual language, and the flow through FOMO's different offerings. That covers the enterprise offices, the all-inclusive workspaces, meetings and conferences, the Soi 21 food court, FOMO Design, and the Slingshot accelerator. My manager reviewed the work and gave direction along the way, but the day-to-day design decisions were mine.

The approach leaned on FOMO's real strengths. Big, confident use of the actual spaces through photography and video. A cleaner structure that lets each part of the ecosystem stand on its own instead of competing for attention. And a tone that reads premium without feeling stiff, closer to how the place actually feels in person.

Working principle

Let the spaces do the talking. The best asset FOMO has is the room itself, so the design mostly needs to frame it well and get out of the way.

03 · Reflection

Real work,
real deadlines

This one moved fast. We were shipping rather than documenting, so this is not a tidy story of research and validated decisions. It is real client work made under real time pressure, and the honest version of that is more useful than a reconstructed one.

The site is live and still being built out, so treat this as a snapshot of an ongoing project rather than a finished artifact. The best place to see where it stands right now is the live site.

What I would add next

More time would mean documenting decisions as they happen, and a simple way to check whether the new design actually moves enquiries and tours. That is the piece this project did not have.
